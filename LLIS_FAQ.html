Structure & Overview

Structure

European Classes is a new branch of education in the Luxembourgish state-school system comprising Kindergarten, Primary and Secondary School. Their aim is to provide a multilingual and multicultural education of high quality, within the Luxembourgish national system.

Our European Classes make part of Lënster Lycée International School in Junglinster (LLIS), Luxembourg, where they started in September 2018.

Although part of the Luxembourgish state educational offer, the European Classes follow the European School structure, syllabi and curriculum in general. LLIS is an Accredited European School and leads its students towards the European Baccalaureate (EB) Diploma.

More information: www.eursc.eu and Accredited European Schools.

  • Kindergarten — 2 years, ages 4–6
  • Primary — 5 years (P1–P5), ages 6–11
  • Secondary — 7 years (S1–S7), ages 11/12–18/19

Age groups are standard minimums. For a variety of schooling reasons, it is not unusual for some pupils to be a full year older than mentioned above.

  • Anglophone Section — Kindergarten, Primary P1–P5, Secondary S1–S7
  • Germanophone Section — Kindergarten, Primary P1–P5, Secondary S1–S7
  • Francophone Section — Kindergarten, Primary P1–P5, Secondary S1–S6

The school follows a system of equivalence with different educational providers from Luxembourg, Europe and worldwide. Previous academic qualifications are preponderant to age.

For a pupil coming from the Luxembourgish primary system, they should have completed the 6th year of Primary (level 4.2) before joining S1 in Secondary school.

Curriculum

Curriculum

In S1–S3 all students follow the same broad curriculum: Language 1, Language 2, Language 3, Mathematics, Integrated Science, Human Sciences, Ethics, Art, Music, Physical Education, ICT and a weekly Mentor Lesson.

Latin is available as an option from S2 onwards. If chosen, it must be continued until at least the end of S3. Students who take Latin do not study ICT in S3.

For a detailed weekly timetable, see the S1–S7 Curriculum Guide.

From S4, the sciences split into Biology, Chemistry and Physics. Students keep their three languages and Mathematics (4 or 6 periods/week), and choose at least one elective subject from Economics, Language 4, Latin, ICT, Music or Art.

History, Geography and Ethics are now taught in Language 2. Luxembourgish may be chosen as an optional subject from S4 at beginner or advanced level.

All syllabi are available on the official European Schools website: www.eursc.eu → Studies → Syllabuses.

Peer and self-evaluation is a fundamental element of our teaching philosophy. We encourage pupils to take learning into their own hands as early as possible. Self-reflection is recorded at the start of the school year and per trimester.

Pupils set target predictions in different subjects, track their progress, and reflect when improvement is needed. They also develop a friendly critical spirit through structured argument-based debate. Parental encouragement in this process is warmly welcomed.

Note: peer and self-evaluation is clearly distinct from formal assessment, which remains the responsibility of subject teachers as course leaders.

Languages

Languages

Three language sections: Anglophone, Germanophone and Francophone.

A linguistic section is defined by the dominant language used for education in that section. In the Anglophone section, for example, pupils study English as their main language — so the section language should be at mother tongue level (or equivalent).

Language 2 (obligatory throughout Secondary) and Language 3 are separate subjects and gain ever-greater importance as pupils progress. Students joining S1 should have a reasonable L2 level (A1/A2 desired). From S3, Human Science subjects are taught in L2. Language 3 begins as a beginner subject in S1 and is obligatory until S5, though it can be continued as an option in the Baccalaureate Cycle.

Yes. Luxembourgish is an obligatory subject of 2 periods per week for all pupils in Primary and Secondary (S1–S3). From S4 onwards it becomes an optional subject choice.

Additionally, European Classes students share the same school environment as national Luxembourgish classes — naturally increasing their exposure to the language.

Yes, through the SWALS model (Students Without A Language Section). This provision allows eligible students to study their own language as L1 (4–5 periods/week at mother tongue level) while attending all other subjects in one of the three sections in their L2.

This aims to support families who have recently arrived in Luxembourg. Creation of SWALS L1 classes depends on demand and may be subject to number restrictions.

Language 1 cannot be changed — it is carefully determined at entry and remains fixed for the duration of studies towards the European Baccalaureate.

L2 and L3 changes are possible at the end of S1, S2, S3 or S5 — not between S4–S5 or S6–S7, and not during the school year.

Parents must apply preferably by end of March of the current school year. A level test takes place in early June; the Class Council then makes a decision based on test results and motivation. The decision is confirmed in the end-of-year report.

  • L1 — language of the section; most subjects taught in L1
  • L2 — from S3: Human Sciences and Ethics; from S4: History, Geography, Ethics and Economics
  • L3 — studied as a beginner language from S1, obligatory to S5
  • Luxembourgish — obligatory S1–S3, optional from S4

Full syllabi: www.eursc.eu

Assessment & Reports

Assessment
  • S1–S3 — letter grades (A, B, C, D, E, Fx). Pass grade: E.
  • S4–S5 — numerical marks (0–10) including half marks. Pass grade: 5. Composed of an A-mark (formative) and B-mark (summative).
  • S6–S7 (Baccalaureate) — marks with one decimal point (0.0–10.0). The overall EB grade is expressed as a percentage; 50% is a pass.

Each report contains a written comment from every subject teacher. Reports are issued per trimester or semester. Each syllabus contains attainment descriptors for each sub-cycle.
